### My Journey into Tech
My journey into the world of technology began in 2012, during my middle school years in Plattsburg, Missouri—a quiet town of just 1,200 people. Living in such a rural area, with my nearest neighbors being my grandparents a mile away, I was surrounded by wide-open spaces, horses roaming our property, and a sense of isolation. It was a peaceful life, but also a lonely one.

For me, making games was more than just a hobby—it was my way of connecting with the outside world. My father, who worked in the U.S. Army Special Forces, was often deployed overseas for months at a time. Games became a bridge between us, allowing us to connect over Skype and share experiences despite the physical distance. My father was incredibly supportive of my tech journey, encouraging my interests and providing me with material he used at work. He handed down old laptops for me to use as servers and development devices, and even bought me my first Raspberry Pi. His support was instrumental in fueling my passion for technology.

On the other hand, my mother wasn’t as supportive. She didn’t see it as a viable career path, and despite her disapproval and attempts to steer me away from technology—going as far as taking away my laptop—I remained determined. I adapted by switching from C# to Ruby, using the MacBook provided by my school to continue coding. This transition led me into the world of web development, where I became an administrator on several online forums, responsible for theming, coding bots, and adding new features. I also began offering web development services to a print shop, two cafés, and a local non-profit.

By 2017, I had started accepting money from clients, developing products like a document server, a VR demo, and an online ordering system that eventually grew into a platform called Order Coffee Online. I made my own salary, worked my own hours, and developed something that both my clients and I were passionate about. I also started side ventures with friends, including weather apps that used computer vision to identify threatening radar signatures like hook and bow echoes, and platforms for locals to make connections across sprawling communities like in Kansas City.

As COVID-19 loomed on the horizon, I saw the potential risks to my business. I sold OCO with the belief that it would shutter by the end of the year due to the pandemic's impact on my client base. I continued to maintain the platform remotely while navigating the uncertainty of the situation. Eventually, a friend offered to buy it, resolving a chapter I had built so passionately.

Shortly thereafter, I joined VisualLive.
### From VisualLive to Unity and Beyond
At VisualLive, I inherited a platform that was in dire need of attention. The system had been passed between various consultants, resulting in a fragmented codebase and painfully slow performance—some index pages took minutes to load. I rolled up my sleeves and got to work, spending weekends refactoring the back-end to eliminate technical debt and optimize performance. Within a short period, I reduced response times by 50% and cut error rates by 92%, transforming VisualLive into a stable, high-performing platform.

My work didn’t go unnoticed, and when VisualLive was acquired by Unity Technologies in 2020, I was brought on to help integrate VisualLive’s innovations into Unity’s cloud ecosystem. My focus at Unity was on streamlining processes and enhancing the user experience. I developed the Status Tracking API, which allowed users to tag data to specific parts of building plans and 3D models, revolutionizing how construction sites were visualized and managed.

By 2023, I had gained a reputation as a problem-solver who could bring order to chaos and create systems that were both powerful and intuitive. My passion for solving problems and building interactive systems continued to drive my work, and I began to explore new frontiers in AI and immersive training scenarios.

In August 2023, I joined Syntreno, a seed-stage startup focused on using AI to assist in soft skills training. I developed their flagship product, an advanced training simulation that used GPT-4 to control lifelike, voice-interactive virtual characters. This product significantly improved learning outcomes by providing users with a highly realistic and immersive training experience. I also engineered a secure API proxy server and collaborated with the CEO and CTO to optimize real-time voice recognition powered by Whisper, resulting in a 50% reduction in latency and an overall enhancement of user satisfaction.

My journey from a self-taught programmer from rural Missouri to a lead software engineer at the forefront of AI and immersive technology has been driven by a relentless pursuit of innovation and a passion for creating systems that make life easier and more enjoyable. As I continue to explore new challenges and opportunities, I remain committed to pushing the boundaries of what technology can achieve and making a meaningful impact on the lives of those who use it.
